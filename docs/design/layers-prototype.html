Design direction · kraken-hub, part two

Kraken Layers

The Kraken stops showing a mode and starts showing a stack: one base — a video, an effect, a picture — with overlays on top. Stats over your lava lamp is the same gesture as stats over YouTube. Every new module for the foreseeable future is just another layer that obeys the same contract, so the home screen never grows another tab.

What changed vs ESC

  • The canvas is the halo, and it's big. ESC edits a 640px face inside a 250px thumbnail. Here the mirror is the largest thing in the window; 1:1 and 2× a wheel away.
  • Guides that know it's a circle. Centre crosshair, safe ring, neighbour edges, rotation ticks — shown only while dragging, gone when you let go. New layers land in free space, never a pile at the centre.
  • Five controls, not thirty-six. Select something and get its essentials; numbers and fonts wait behind More. Undo/redo up top — ESC has none.
  • The budget is the limit. No "40 elements": the meter reads the real frame rate and names the heaviest layer when a stack is too rich.

What changed

  • Modes become a stack. The chips that used to switch modules now pick the base; overlays sit above it with a switch each. One list, top to bottom, is the whole configuration.
  • "Add a layer" is the only new gesture. Stats, clock, text, a second effect — the same sheet. Nothing else on the home screen changes, so the control budget holds.
  • The frame budget is visible. The Kraken compresses every frame; a busy stack costs real FPS. The bar reads the governor's achieved rate and the app says when a stack is too rich instead of silently stuttering.
  • It's what CAM can't do. CAM overlays stats on GIFs only. Video and effects are ours.

What changed

  • One hero number. A face leads with the figure you glance for, ≥48px in the system face, and nothing competes with it. Meters are thin arcs with rounded ends; the track is a lighter step of the same hue so state reads across the whole ring.
  • Warm means something. The accent never changes hue with the value. Past a threshold the ring turns amber with a word ("Hot"), never colour alone.
  • Round-native. Dual mirrors two half-arcs; Triple is concentric rings; Overlay is a scrim along the bottom of the circle, built to sit over video without eating the frame rate.
  • Every metric the API has. CPU / GPU temp, load, clock, liquid, RAM, fan, power — CAM's own faces stop at seven.

What changed

  • "Effects," not shaders. The word never appears. An effect is a thing you pick, a preset is a look you pick, Custom is four colours.
  • Your materials are the presets. Every banner shipped nine colour variants; those become the preset chips — Sunset, Ocean, Forest, Rose, Grape, Ember, Mono, Sun — no colour design work needed.
  • Temperature-reactive. Effects can take the CPU temperature as an input: the palette warms as the chip does. Off by default, one switch to turn on.
  • Low-entropy first. Lava lamp, nebula, satin and interference are slow and smooth — exactly what the compressor likes at 40 fps. Fire and fine-noise effects come later with a "detail" control.

Layers, not modes

One contract for every visual thing: render into a rect, declare a cost hint, expose a config. The home screen composes; it never grows.

Readable from the couch

Faces lead with one number, use one hue for magnitude, and reserve warm colours for thresholds — with a word, never colour alone.

Effects, not shaders

Pick an effect, pick a preset, or set four colours. The technology stays behind the curtain.

The frame budget is honest

The Kraken compresses frames; the app measures what you actually get and tells you when a stack costs too much.

Implementation notes. The layer contract extends the existing module registry (a module becomes a layer: render(rect, ctx), cost, overlayCapable); the display composites a stack in one frame clock and the config exposes it as the list above — activeModuleId migrates to layers[]. Stats faces reuse the monitoring feed, tween between 1 Hz samples, and draw with flat fills only. Effects are one WebGL2 fullscreen quad with a fixed uniform contract (uTime, uResolution, uColor0–3, uTemp) — every Tier-1 banner fits it unchanged.
