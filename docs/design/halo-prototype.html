Design direction · kraken-hub

Halo for Kraken

The current app is organized like the codebase — Setup, Sources, Remote, Diagnostics. This redesign organizes it around the object: your Kraken's round screen sits front and center, always live, and every control is something you do to it. One primary action per screen; everything technical earns its place or disappears behind disclosure.

What changed

  • The screen is the interface. A live mirror of the LCD replaces the Preview tab — you never wonder what your cooler is showing.
  • One primary action. Paste a link, press Play. Detection, download, quality — all automatic, reported after the fact.
  • Modules are a choice, not a settings page. Four chips under the screen; tap to switch, options appear contextually beneath.
  • Six tabs → zero. Remote pairing, the clip library and diagnostics live behind one quiet disclosure. The FPS-slider warning appears only when CAM's slider is actually low — we can read it.

What changed

  • The magic is legible. “Saving to your Kraken” tells the download-first story in plain words — the app's biggest differentiator used to happen silently.
  • No provider jargon. Users never see “adapter”, “geometry engine” or “resolver” — they see what happened and what they got.
  • States, not spinners. Every wait names what's happening and what comes next; failures say how to fix, in the same voice.

The remote is a media remote — not a settings mirror

What changed

  • Thumb-first. Transport controls sized and placed for one hand; the loop is two draggable handles on the scrubber, not number fields.
  • Trust at a glance. The mini-halo mirrors the LCD via live telemetry, so you know the phone and the cooler agree.
  • Pairing is one card. Scan the QR shown in CAM once; the room code becomes a quiet credential, not a feature.

Moments, not modules

Screens map to what people do — glance, change, tweak, control from the couch — never to how the code is layered.

Disclosure over density

Diagnostics, transports, endpoints and per-provider caveats exist — one level down, exactly where the curious will look.

Motion with physics

One spring curve everywhere (cubic-bezier .32,.72,0,1), 200–450 ms, honored by prefers-reduced-motion. Nothing bounces for fun.

The platform's own type

The shipped app uses the system font — SF on Mac, Segoe in CAM — which is how native-feeling apps actually do it.

Implementation notes. This maps cleanly onto the shipped architecture: the live mirror is the existing simulator surface fed by real state; module chips drive the existing registry; the paste flow wraps the existing detect → download → mount pipeline and its telemetry; the remote reuses the sync channel as-is. Config-UI bundle budget is 140 KB with ~20 KB used — plenty of room. Nothing on the LCD's render path changes.
